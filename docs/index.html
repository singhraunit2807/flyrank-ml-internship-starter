Predicting Content Decline with Client-Held-Out Validation

Raunit Singh · Machine Learning · FlyRank ML Internship · ML-11 Capstone
Abstract. This paper asks whether page-level search and content signals can help identify content that is already associated with decline. The executed analysis uses the anonymized FlyRank teaching dataset: 30,000 content rows from 32 clients. A Random Forest is evaluated with a client-grouped holdout so test clients are completely unseen during training. A naive row split produced ROC AUC 0.763 and Average Precision 0.776, while the client-grouped split produced ROC AUC 0.600 and Average Precision 0.587. The main practical output is a ranked human-review queue, not an automatic content decision system.

1. Introduction and Problem Statement

The practical question is: Can a machine-learning model help identify pages that deserve human review for decline risk, while still being evaluated honestly across clients?

This is a decision-support problem. The goal is not to claim that the model has discovered Google's ranking algorithm, nor that a particular page characteristic causes decline. The model is used to prioritize pages for investigation and to make the review process more consistent.

The methodological question is equally important: how much does measured performance change when validation is performed on completely unseen clients instead of randomly splitting individual rows?

3. Methodology

Label and leakage control

The target is derived from trend_direction. Therefore trend_direction and trend_pct are excluded from model features. The generated label itself, is_declining_label, is also excluded. Identifiers content_id and client_id are excluded because they are identifiers rather than predictive content signals and could create leakage or memorization.

Model

The final model is a Random Forest classifier with 300 trees, min_samples_leaf=5, class_weight="balanced_subsample", and random_state=42. Features cover search, traffic, engagement, content-age, freshness, and categorical content signals.

Validation design

The primary validation keeps all rows belonging to a client together. Approximately 20% of clients are held out for testing. The seven test clients have zero client overlap with the training set. A naive random row split is shown only as a diagnostic comparison because 31 clients appeared on both sides.

The random split gives higher scores, but it is not a clean new-client test because the same clients occur in both training and testing. The client-grouped result is therefore the main validation result used for interpretation.

5. Research Findings and Interpretation

Content lifecycle

The source analysis reports that growing pages were younger on average than declining pages, approximately 185 days versus 228 days, while average word count was nearly the same. This is an observed association in the analyzed cohort. It does not establish that younger content causes growth.

Freshness

The source analysis reports a 31–90 day freshness-window growth-to-decline ratio of 5.43:1 and a separate refreshed-versus-stale comparison for older content. These are observed cohort comparisons. Selection effects and other group differences cannot be ruled out from those comparisons alone.

7. Limitations and Honest Framing

  1. The executed model evaluation is based on 30,000 anonymized rows, not an independent run over the full hosted 79M-row warehouse.
  2. The client-grouped test contains seven held-out clients, so results should not be generalized to every possible future client without further validation.
  3. Model scores indicate estimated class risk, not causation.
  4. The research comparisons describe associations and cohort differences rather than causal effects.
  5. The dataset used here does not provide real business costs, so the action queue does not claim measured financial ROI.
  6. High-impact content changes require human review and post-change measurement.
  7. The random-split numbers are included to demonstrate the validation sensitivity, not as the primary new-client performance estimate.
